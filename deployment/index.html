The Problem

Anticipating, not reacting.

A pedestrian stepping off a curb at 30 mph is already inside the vehicle's stopping distance. A reactive system that waits until the pedestrian enters the road has already missed its chance.

Autonomous vehicles need to predict crossing intent before it happens, from a single camera frame, in real time, at the edge of the sensor pipeline.

Task: given a single 300×300 RGB crop centered on a pedestrian, predict will cross or won't cross, with no video, no LIDAR, and no temporal context.

We reproduced EfficientPIE (Qu et al., IJCAI 2025), currently one of the fastest intent classifiers at 0.21 ms/frame, on the JAAD benchmark dataset, then asked three questions about it:

  • Does it fail fairly across demographic groups?
  • Does it behave differently at different pedestrian scales?
  • Is it actually looking at the pedestrian, or just reading the scene?

Model

EfficientPIE Architecture

EfficientPIE (Qu et al., IJCAI 2025) is a purely convolutional, single-frame classifier with no recurrent components. It operates on a cropped 300×300 RGB image of the pedestrian and outputs a crossing / not-crossing decision in 0.21 ms.

Design Choices

  • Depthwise separable convolutions throughout, reducing compute to roughly 1/9 the cost of standard convolutions.
  • Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention in MBConv blocks for improved representational capacity.
  • No recurrent components. The design is single-frame only; no temporal context is required.
  • The paper also applies IDIL (Intention Domain Incremental Learning) and Progressive Perturbation during training, contributing roughly 5–6% accuracy improvement over the base model.

Our Investigation

Four-Part Analysis

Rather than simply verifying the paper's reported accuracy, we ran a structured robustness study: first reproducing the baseline, then probing how and where it fails.

01

Baseline Reproduction: Accuracy Can Mislead

We reproduced EfficientPIE on the JAAD test split (1,876 samples: 1,584 non-crossing, 292 crossing). The headline accuracy of 88.3% looks strong, but what's hiding behind it tells a different story.

A model that ignores every crossing pedestrian still achieves ~84% accuracy on this split due to the 5.4:1 class imbalance. Our model scores 88.3%, but achieves only 64% recall on the crossing class. It misses 1 in 3 pedestrians about to cross.

In a safety-critical system, 64% recall on the dangerous class is the number that matters, not 88.3% overall accuracy.

Demographic Bias Analysis: Failure Is Environmental

We stratified errors across pedestrian attributes: age group (adult / young / senior / child), gender, and group size. None of the inter-group performance gaps were statistically significant.

The failure mode is environmental, not demographic. The model doesn't fail based on who the pedestrian is. It fails based on where they are relative to the camera and what surrounds them. Ruling out demographic bias pointed us directly at scale and context as the culprits.

Two findings, one cause.

The scale paradox and the occlusion ablation tell the same story.

SCALE PARADOX

Closest pedestrians fail with highest confidence

When the pedestrian fills the frame, surrounding scene context is cropped out of the 300×300 input. The model has no background signal to work with and collapses. Accuracy drops 18 points in Q5 despite the model reporting peak confidence.

SPATIAL ABLATION + GRAD-CAM

The model reads the scene, not the person

Removing the pedestrian body drops F1 to near chance (0.064). Keeping only the background preserves 70% of baseline F1. Grad-CAM++ confirms this visually: the pedestrian is always cold, the background always hot.

↓

Root cause: EfficientPIE has learned to classify crossing intent by reading static background cues, not pedestrian body language or intent signals. The scene shortcuts work in most cases but break down when context is missing (large/close crops) or when the correct answer requires understanding the pedestrian.

Hypothesis: if we force the model to align its internal representations with a scene-aware teacher that has access to the full uncropped frame, it may learn more principled scene semantics, reducing reliance on brittle, crop-dependent shortcuts. Enter VLM Knowledge Distillation.

Our Approach

VLM Knowledge Distillation

We used CLIP ViT-B/32 as an offline teacher. CLIP processes the full, uncropped JAAD frames, giving it access to complete scene context the student never sees. The teacher's embeddings are extracted once offline; at inference time, only the student runs.

The student (EfficientPIE) gets a 1280→1024→768 MLP projector attached to its bottleneck feature vector. During training, the projector is penalized for not matching the CLIP embedding of the corresponding full scene.

Outcome

Accuracy, F1, and Recall were unchanged after distillation. Grad-CAM++ shows a small but visible increase in activation around the pedestrian's feet and lower body, a sign that the distillation signal nudged the model in the right direction.

However, it isn't enough. The increase in pedestrian-region activation is too weak to meaningfully shift what drives the prediction. CLIP's general image–text pretraining can be satisfied by background scene templates alone, so the student never has to learn pedestrian-level attention to minimize the distillation loss. A pedestrian-grounded teacher is needed.

What's next

Future Work

01

Pedestrian-Grounded VLM Distillation

Replace CLIP with a domain-aware VLM (e.g., LLaVA) prompted with pedestrian-specific questions: "Is this person at a crosswalk?", "Are they facing the road?", "Are they near a curb?". A pedestrian-grounded teacher produces a distillation signal the student cannot satisfy with background context alone, while preserving real-time inference speed.

02

Scene-Balanced Dataset

Ensure each filming location appears in both crossing and non-crossing conditions. This decorrelates scene type from crossing label, eliminating the environment-intent correlation that EfficientPIE currently exploits. The model would be forced to find genuine behavioral signal.
